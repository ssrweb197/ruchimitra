SMALL TIPS · BETTER FLAVOUR

Kitchen Tips

Simple, useful guidance for everyday South Indian cooking.

🫙

Podi storage

  • Cool roasted ingredients completely before grinding and packing.
  • Use a clean, completely dry, airtight container.
  • Always use a dry spoon; keep damp hands and moisture away.
  • Refrigerate mixes containing fresh garlic, coconut or other moist ingredients; follow a tested recipe's storage guidance.
🔥

Spice roasting

  • Roast on low to medium heat so the spices warm evenly without burning.
  • Stir frequently and remove each ingredient when fragrant.
  • Roast ingredients separately when their sizes or cooking times differ.
  • Cool fully before grinding; warm ingredients can create condensation.
⚖️

Kitchen measurements

  • 1 teaspoon = about 5 ml; 1 tablespoon = about 15 ml.
  • 1 metric cup = 240 ml; use the same cup throughout a recipe.
  • Grams measure weight; millilitres measure volume. They are not interchangeable for every ingredient.
  • For repeatable results, use a kitchen scale for dry ingredients.
🍲

Everyday cooking tips

  • Prepare and measure ingredients before switching on the stove.
  • Add salt and chilli gradually, tasting as you go.
  • Keep tempering heat controlled; burnt spices can make a dish bitter.
  • For pickles, use a tested recipe and follow its exact salt, oil, hygiene and storage instructions.

Tip: Storage time depends on ingredients, moisture, hygiene and temperature. When in doubt, refrigerate and discard food showing mould, off-odours or other spoilage.

Cooking Guides & Kitchen Know-how

Practical guides to help you cook with confidence, plan quantities and care for ingredients.

🌶️ Podi Guide

How to Make Flavourful South Indian Podi at Home

Choose fresh ingredients, roast each one gently and cool everything before grinding. Add salt and spice gradually, then adjust to your taste.

Read cooking guide
A good podi starts with dry, clean ingredients. Roast dals until aromatic and lightly golden; roast sesame and spices separately because they cook at different speeds. Avoid high heat, which can make spices bitter. Let ingredients cool completely, grind in short pulses and store in a clean, dry, airtight jar. The exact roasting time depends on the ingredient, pan and heat.
🫙 Storage Guide

How to Store Homemade Spice Powders Safely

Moisture, heat and light can reduce quality. Use dry tools, small batches and storage advice suited to the ingredients.

Read storage guide
Allow freshly prepared powders to cool fully before closing the jar. Keep the container away from steam, direct sunlight and the stove. Always use a dry spoon. Blends with fresh coconut, fresh garlic or other moist ingredients need more cautious handling and refrigeration; follow a tested recipe's storage instructions. Discard food if you notice mould, unusual odours or other signs of spoilage. There is no single safe shelf-life for every homemade mix.
⚖️ Measurement Guide

Kitchen Measurement Conversions for Indian Cooking

Keep measurements consistent: teaspoons and tablespoons measure volume, while grams measure weight.

Read measurement guide
As a common kitchen reference, 1 metric teaspoon is about 5 ml, 1 tablespoon is about 15 ml and 1 metric cup is 240 ml. These are volume measures. A cup of flour, rice, peanuts or grated coconut will weigh different amounts, so do not convert ml to grams without ingredient-specific information. For repeatable recipes, weigh dry ingredients with a kitchen scale and use the same measuring set each time.
🍲 Cooking Guide

Simple Ways to Balance Salt, Spice and Sourness

Taste in stages and make small adjustments. A little at a time is easier to correct than adding too much at once.

Read cooking guide
Add salt in stages, especially when ingredients will reduce during cooking. Add chilli gradually and allow it to cook briefly before deciding whether more is needed. Tamarind, tomato and lemon vary in sourness, so taste before adding the full amount. If a dish becomes too spicy, increasing the overall quantity with suitable unsalted ingredients may help; the best fix depends on the recipe. Record adjustments that work so you can repeat them next time.
